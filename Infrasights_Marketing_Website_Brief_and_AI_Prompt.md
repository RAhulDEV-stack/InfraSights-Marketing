## 2. The problem Infrasights addresses

SAN environments are critical, but understanding what is happening
across them can be difficult when information is spread across devices,
tools, screens, and operational workflows.

Common operational challenges include:

-   **Fragmented visibility:** Operators may need to inspect multiple
    switches and interfaces to understand the broader environment.
-   **Time-consuming investigation:** Finding the relevant device, port,
    or operational detail can involve repeated manual steps.
-   **Limited at-a-glance understanding:** Teams need a clear view of
    infrastructure status rather than disconnected technical outputs.
-   **Operational complexity:** SAN environments can become harder to
    understand as infrastructure grows.
-   **Knowledge concentration:** Troubleshooting can depend heavily on
    people who know where to look and how to interpret device
    information.

Infrasights should be marketed around reducing this visibility and
investigation burden. Avoid promising that it eliminates outages or
automatically resolves every SAN issue.

## 3. Product value proposition

### Centralized SAN visibility

Bring SAN switch information into a unified product experience, instead
of making operators piece together the environment manually.

### Direct switch data collection

Infrasights collects data directly from SAN switches. This is the
current architecture and should be reflected consistently in all
diagrams, copy, and website content.

### Faster operational understanding

Present infrastructure information in a way that helps teams understand
what they are looking at and where they may need to investigate.

### A clearer operational workflow

Give SAN and storage teams a common place to review infrastructure
information and support their operational work.

### Deployment as a product engagement

Let interested organizations request an Infrasights deployment through
the website. A deployment request should lead into a secure account
registration or login journey, followed by a structured request process.

-   SAN administrators

-   Data center infrastructure teams
-   Infrastructure operations teams
-   Enterprise IT operations leaders
-   Managed infrastructure and storage service providers

### Buyer and evaluator personas

**Technical evaluator --- SAN / Storage Administrator**

Wants to understand data collection, supported infrastructure,
visibility, operational usefulness, access requirements, and deployment
effort.

**Operational owner --- Infrastructure Manager**

Wants better visibility across the SAN environment, a consistent
operational view, and a product that can fit into the organization's
operating model.

**Business approver --- IT Director / Head of Infrastructure**

Wants to understand the value, deployment approach, security posture,
support model, and commercial engagement.

Website content should serve all three audiences: clear business value
first, technical depth available when needed.

### Messaging guardrails

The following are positioning directions, not claims of
already-implemented features. Only publish them as factual product
capabilities after they have been verified in the product:

-   Real-time or near-real-time monitoring
-   Automated alerts, notifications, or event correlation
-   Predictive analytics or anomaly detection
-   AI-powered root-cause analysis
-   Automated remediation
-   Capacity forecasting
-   Historical trend analysis
-   Multi-tenancy or multi-customer management
-   Role-based access control, SSO, or audit logs
-   Specific vendor, switch model, firmware, or protocol support
-   High availability, scalability, or quantified performance guarantees
-   Cloud, on-premises, or hybrid deployment options

Do not invent customer logos, testimonials, certifications, compliance
claims, uptime statistics, quantified savings, integrations, or
performance figures. Use clearly marked placeholders until approved
evidence is available.

## 7. Deployment request and authentication journey

The website should make the deployment CTA prominent, but the request
should be associated with an authenticated user.

### Recommended user journey

``` mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Visitor opens Infrasights website"] --> B["Clicks Request a Deployment"]
    B --> C{"Authenticated?"}
    C -->|"No"| D["Register or Login"]
    D --> E["Complete authentication"]
    C -->|"Yes"| F["Open Deployment Request"]
    E --> F
    F --> G["Complete deployment questionnaire"]
    G --> H["Review request details"]
    H --> I["Submit deployment request"]
    I --> J["Show confirmation and request reference"]
    J --> K["Internal team reviews request"]
    K --> L["Contact requester and plan next steps"]
```

### Registration fields

Keep registration simple:

-   Full name
-   Business email
-   Organization
-   Password
-   Terms and privacy consent

Use email verification and secure password handling if these are part of
the implemented authentication system.

### Login

-   Business email
-   Password
-   Forgot password
-   Link to registration

### Deployment questionnaire

Collect only information needed to qualify and plan the deployment.
Suggested fields:

**Organization** - Organization name - Business email - Country /
region - Role / department

**SAN environment** - SAN vendor - Switch models - Approximate number of
switches - Fabric count - Current firmware versions, if known

**Deployment expectations** - Preferred deployment model (options should
reflect what Infrasights actually supports) - Approximate target
timeline - Environment notes - Key objectives or challenges

**Contact** - Preferred contact method - Additional comments

Mark nonessential technical fields as optional. Do not ask users to
submit switch passwords, SNMP community strings, private keys, or other
infrastructure secrets through a marketing form.

### After submission

Show a clear confirmation page:

**"Your deployment request has been submitted."**

Explain that the Infrasights team will review the information and
contact the requester. Do not promise a response time until an actual
service-level commitment has been established.

The authenticated area may show request status and history if that
functionality is built.

## 8. Visual identity and motion direction

### Overall visual direction

Aim for a premium enterprise infrastructure / observability product
aesthetic:

-   Dark navy or charcoal foundation
-   Electric cyan, teal, or restrained blue accents
-   High-contrast typography
-   Clean spacing and strong visual hierarchy
-   Subtle gradients and restrained glow effects
-   Technical diagrams and product imagery
-   A polished, trustworthy enterprise feel

The site should feel modern and technical, not like a gaming interface
or a generic animated startup template.

### Motion graphics concept

**Hero: Living SAN topology**

Show a network of switch nodes connected by fine lines. Small packets or
pulses travel along the connections. The network gradually resolves into
a clean, structured topology, communicating that Infrasights turns
infrastructure data into a clearer view.

**Data collection animation**

Show a SAN switch sending data directly into the Infrasights collection
layer, then through processing and into the platform. Make the direct
connection explicit. Do not insert SANnav into the animation.

**Visibility transformation**

Start with scattered device indicators and disconnected information
panels. Transition into one coherent operational dashboard concept.

**Scroll storytelling**

As the visitor scrolls: - Architecture nodes appear progressively. -
Connection lines draw into view. - Capability cards reveal with subtle
movement. - Dashboard panels move gently into position.

**Micro-interactions** - Smooth button hover states - Subtle card
elevation - Animated line drawing - Small status indicator pulses -
Gentle number transitions only for real metrics

### Motion and accessibility rules

-   Keep animation purposeful and restrained.
-   Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
-   Do not make important information available only through animation.
-   Avoid constant flashing, rapid movement, or distracting parallax.
-   Optimize animations for mobile and lower-powered devices.
-   Prefer CSS transforms and opacity for lightweight motion; use canvas
    or WebGL only where justified.

## 10. Website quality, security, and implementation requirements

### Product and content integrity

-   Use Infrasights as the product name throughout.
-   Mention InsightOps only where a legacy-name reference is genuinely
    needed.
-   Show direct SAN switch data collection.
-   Do not depict SANnav as a required component.
-   Distinguish live product screenshots from concept visuals.
-   Do not invent capabilities or customer proof.

### Security and privacy

-   Use secure authentication practices.
-   Validate all form inputs on the server.
-   Protect authenticated routes.
-   Apply rate limiting and abuse protection to authentication and
    public forms.
-   Use CSRF protection where applicable to the chosen architecture.
-   Store passwords using a modern password-hashing algorithm; never
    store plaintext passwords.
-   Do not collect infrastructure credentials in the marketing
    deployment form.
-   Provide a privacy notice and terms appropriate to the business and
    jurisdictions served.
-   Restrict access to deployment requests to authorized internal
    personnel.

### Conversion and usability

-   Keep Request a Deployment visible in the navigation.
-   Make the CTA clear on desktop and mobile.
-   Keep registration and request forms focused.
-   Explain what happens after submission.
-   Provide useful validation and error states.
-   Ensure the site is responsive and keyboard accessible.

### Search and sharing

-   Add page titles and meta descriptions.
-   Use semantic headings and meaningful link text.
-   Add Open Graph metadata for social sharing.
-   Use descriptive alt text for meaningful images.
-   Optimize image sizes and loading.
-   Include a sitemap and robots configuration appropriate to the
    production site.

### Analytics

Track meaningful funnel events, subject to the site's privacy and
consent approach:

-   Marketing page view
-   Platform page view
-   Request a Deployment click
-   Registration started / completed
-   Login completed
-   Deployment form started
-   Deployment request submitted

Do not collect passwords, infrastructure secrets, or sensitive form
contents in analytics.
